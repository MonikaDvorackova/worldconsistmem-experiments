7. **Consequence:** the claim that F3 improves *exact-match structured output* stands. The claim that it improves *transition reconstruction* is not supported beyond a tendency, shared with F2, to select the most recent of several documented events.

## 6. F0-F3 re-evaluation

All scenarios apply one rule to all four arms on the same items. 'Exact' is the frozen transition scorer v2. 'Semantic, question-aligned' requires the single stated answer to give every field the question asks for (the time; for 'When and how did the CEO change' also the old and new holder) with the gold value and to contradict no other gold field, ignoring key names, nesting and wrappers. 'Semantic, full tuple' requires every gold field to be recoverable. Abstentions: {'F0': 0, 'F1': 0, 'F2': 0, 'F3': 0}; unparseable responses: {'F0': 0, 'F1': 0, 'F2': 0, 'F3': 0}.

- Restricted to uniquely specified questions, human labels are: F0 17 correct; F1 16 correct, 1 unresolved; F2 17 correct; F3 17 correct (of 17 per arm); no response to a uniquely specified question was judged incorrect by both reviewers. This agrees with the automated semantic result in C2-B1.

## 7. Answers to the evaluation questions

1. **Original scoring:** yes. F3 109/150 vs F0 13, F1 90, F2 39; all three contrasts are large and statistically supported.
2. **Semantic-only scoring:** partly. Question-aligned: F3 113/150 vs F0 91, F1 90, F2 132. F3 remains ahead of F0 and F1 (+14.7 [+6.2, +22.9]; 27 vs 5; p=0.00011) but is behind F2 (-12.7 [-19.7, -6.2]; 4 vs 23; p=0.00031). The whole remaining difference comes from which of several events is picked.
3. **Excluding ambiguous questions:** excluding only the human-established ones changes little (86/140 | 85/140 | 123/140 | 105/140). Excluding every structurally non-unique item removes the semantic advantage completely (74/74 | 74/74 | 74/74 | 74/74) while the exact-match gap persists (10/74 | 74/74 | 12/74 | 74/74).
4. **Sensitivity to unresolved adjudications:** negligible for the 150-item analysis, which does not use human labels. In the human-label table the six unresolved responses move F1 by up to 3 and F3 by up to 2 of 30; no conclusion depends on them. Sensitivity to the treatment of *ambiguous questions* is decisive: the F3-F0 semantic difference ranges from 0.0 pp (lower) through 14.7 pp (benchmark convention) to 4.0 pp (upper).
5. **Schema compliance:** yes, it explains most of the exact-match difference. Of F3's 97 exact-match wins over F0, 64 are on uniquely specified questions where F0's answer is semantically correct but lacks unrequested fields (for example a bare timestamp for a 'When' question). The F0 prompt contains only `schema=transition_tuple` and never lists the gold fields.
6. **Defensible claims:** (a) measured exact-match transition accuracy is highly sensitive to the response interface; (b) specifying the output schema accounts for most of that sensitivity; (c) on questions with several documented candidate events, the prev/event/next instruction (F2, F3) shifts the selected event toward the most recent one (13/42 | 16/42 | 38/42 | 35/42); (d) with gold-support recall 1, GPT-4.1 named a documented candidate event in 144-150 of 150 items in every arm.
7. **Claims to weaken or withdraw:** (a) that the generic interface 'reconstructs only 13/150 transitions' as a statement about reconstruction ability; (b) that 0 format-only wins shows the gain is not format: the M5 audit did not cover unrequested fields; (c) that the 0/34 document floor is a reconstruction or entailment failure; (d) that schema and representation 'both contribute' to reconstruction: schema contributes to contract compliance, representation to latest-event selection; (e) any human-validated accuracy estimate, which does not yet exist.

## 8. Limitations

- One model snapshot (gpt-4.1-2025-04-14), temperature 0, one run per item, synthetic worlds, 18 worlds.
- The semantic scorer is a deterministic rule set written after the results were known. It was checked against human labels only on the 44 jointly labelled Round 2 responses and the unique-question subset of Round 1; it is not a substitute for human grading of all 600 responses.
- Structural uniqueness is judged against the retrieved pack. In 24 CEO/ownership items and 24 document items the full world history contains more events than the pack.
- 'Selects the latest event' is agreement with a benchmark convention, not correctness.
- Human labels: two reviewers, no notes, no sufficiency ratings, 6 unresolved, 22 Round 1 labels on non-unique questions not re-reviewed, reviewer identity across rounds unknown, independence not verifiable from the files.
- The arm can often be guessed from the answer format (F3 is bare JSON, F2 is prev/event/next), so the human review was not blind to arm in practice.
- No second model, no full human regrade, and no re-run with disambiguated questions has been performed.

## 9. Implications for the paper

1. Report exact-match and question-aligned semantic accuracy side by side, and make the semantic result part of the headline.
2. Reframe the contribution as a measurement-validity finding: evidence availability is not the bottleneck, and exact-match transition scoring mostly measures output-contract compliance.
3. Fix the benchmark: disambiguate the 42 multi-event questions (for example 'most recent'), repair the 34 document questions and their packs, and re-run all arms. Until then report these strata separately.
4. Replace the S1-S5 labels with the three structural strata, or re-derive them after the fix.
5. State the `event` unwrapping used for F2 and the offline parser used for F3.
6. Complete third-reviewer adjudication of the six disagreements and re-review the 22 Round 1 labels on non-unique questions before reporting any human-validated figure.
